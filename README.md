## The four layers people accidentally merge into one question

When an agency says they want "white label AI in HighLevel," they usually mean one of these:

1. **White-labeling the CRM.** HighLevel lets you put your logo and domain on the desktop app so clients log into *your* platform. On the Agency Pro plan ($497/mo) you also get SaaS Mode, which lets you sell the software itself as a subscription with automated account creation.
2. **The AI features inside HighLevel.** Conversation AI, Voice AI, Reviews AI, and the rest run on an add-on: $50/month per sub-account on the AI Employee Growth plan, or $97/month per sub-account for unlimited usage. Pay-per-use is also available at $0.02 per message.
3. **The conversational AI product itself.** This is the piece you'd resell as your own AI service. HighLevel's native AI is a feature inside the platform you already pay for — it isn't a product you rebrand and mark up as your own offering in the way an agency usually wants.
4. **The client-facing dashboard.** If you're charging a client $500/month for "our AI sales team," they need somewhere to log in and see conversations and bookings. HighLevel's sub-account view wasn't designed for that pitch.

Layers 1 and 4 you can solve inside HighLevel. Layer 3 is where most agencies end up looking outside it — because the moment you resell AI as your own product, you need white labeling, per-client rebilling, and margin control that the native AI add-on doesn't give you.

## How the rebilling actually works

This is the part that turns a software expense into a line of revenue, so it's worth being precise.

On the Agency plan, you pay CloseBot and your clients pay you. Each client tops up their wallet through *your* Stripe connection, and you top up yours to pay CloseBot. You set your own markup. There are four separate places you can rebill:

- **Message volume** — the per-message rate you're charged, marked up to whatever you want.
- **Knowledge library storage** — billed to you at $0.006/MB per day, rebillable at your markup. For scale: 1 MB of text is roughly 1,000 pages.
- **User seats** — $5 per seat, including client seats. Set your own price on top.
- **Token costs** — tracked by CloseBot so you can pass some or all of it through if you choose.

Sub-accounts themselves cost nothing extra, and every plan allows unlimited account connections, which is the detail that makes the agency tier viable at 50 clients rather than 5.

Your clients get their own white-labeled portal with their own KPIs — conversation volume, bookings, and their costs — separate from the agency dashboard. That's the retention argument: clients who can see appointments accumulating are harder to churn than clients taking your word for it.

One caveat that shows up in CloseBot's own documentation versus its pricing FAQ: the two pages don't agree on whether you bring your own provider API keys. Since provider token spend is a real part of your cost stack at high volume, confirm the current behavior for your account before you promise a client a fixed price.

## Setting it up: the realistic path

If you're doing this to resell, the order matters.

1. **Create a free account and build one agent end to end.** The free tier lets you see the builder and testing portal before you pay. One agent, one job flow.
2. **Connect a HighLevel sub-account.** It's a click-to-connect flow rather than an API project.
3. **Build the job flow properly.** Qualification branches, booking node on the right calendar, business hours configured so follow-ups don't fire at 2 a.m.
4. **Define variables your clients fill in.** This is the trick that makes one agent work across many clients in the same niche — amenities for a gym, service areas for a contractor. Your clients enter their values in their portal; you keep control of the logic.
5. **Test with real objection scenarios.** Discount requests, rescheduling, "just send me info."
6. **Move to the Agency plan when you're ready to resell.** That's where the white-label portal, client seats, and rebilling unlock. The business plan won't show you rebilling or white labeling, so don't build a reseller pitch on a trial you haven't upgraded.
7. **Set your markup and connect Stripe.** Then decide your client price. Agencies on the platform report everything from around $100/month to five figures monthly per client, which is a very wide range and mostly reflects what the agency is actually selling alongside the AI.

Most teams get their first agent live the same day, per CloseBot. Your mileage depends on how messy your qualification logic is.

## Which plan makes sense for which agency

**Stay on Free** if you're still testing whether AI booking works for your clients' lead flow. 100 messages a month is enough to see whether the conversations hold up.

**Go Business** if you're using AI on your own pipeline — real estate team, home services, healthcare, coaching. Message costs are included in the base price at the entry tier, which makes budgeting straightforward, and you don't need rebilling if nobody's paying you for it.

**Go Agency** the moment you have two or more clients paying you for AI. The $397 flat fee against HighLevel's $97 per sub-account for unlimited AI means the crossover happens fast, and white labeling is the entire point of the exercise. If a client would be annoyed to discover your "proprietary AI sales team" is a tool they could subscribe to themselves, you need the agency plan.

**Go Growth** only for compliance, SLAs, or genuine volume. HIPAA and quarterly audits are the reasons, not the 50+ templates.

Worth being clear about one limit: an AI setter qualifies and books. It doesn't close. If your client expects the bot to negotiate a six-figure contract, no plan tier fixes that, and the vendor comparisons that imply otherwise are selling.
